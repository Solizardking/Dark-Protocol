# Dark Protocol — Ceremony Contributor Packet

**You are being asked to contribute secret entropy to the production Groth16
trusted setup for the Dark Protocol shielded pool's withdraw circuit.**
Everything you need is on this page. You do not need to trust the coordinator,
the other contributors, or this document's claims about the cryptography —
the ceremony is sound if **at least one** contributor (you, for all you need
to care) generates their entropy honestly and destroys it.

Time needed: ~30–60 minutes, on your own machine. **Deadline: 24–48 hours**
from receiving the incoming zkey — the ceremony is sequential, so everyone
after you is waiting on your hop.

---

## What you are contributing to

- Circuit: Dark Protocol `withdraw.circom`, 13,162 constraints, BN254 / Groth16.
- Phase 1 is already settled and public: the Hermez perpetual Powers of Tau
  file `powersOfTau28_hez_final_15.ptau` (54 independent contributions + a
  beacon; BLAKE2b-512 pinned in the ceremony transcript header). Your hop is
  part of **phase 2**, the circuit-specific setup.
- Your contribution re-randomizes the proving key with your secret entropy.
  If your entropy is honestly generated and then destroyed, the setup's
  "toxic waste" can never be reconstructed — even if every other participant
  colluded. If your entropy survives anywhere (a note, a screenshot, a shell
  history), your contribution adds nothing. **Destruction is the job.**

## What you need

- The ceremony kit: `zk/ceremony/` from the Dark Protocol tree
  (`contribute.sh`, `verify-transcript.sh`, `RUNBOOK.md`), plus Node.js and
  the tree's `zk/node_modules` (snarkjs). The coordinator will tell you
  exactly how you received the tree/kit.
- The incoming zkey for your hop (`withdraw_NNNN.zkey`) from the coordinator.
- The published ceremony transcript (URL from the coordinator), which lists
  every previous hop's hashes.

## Step 1 — Verify the incoming zkey BEFORE trusting it

1. Check its sha256 against the value published in the transcript for the
   previous hop:

   ```
   sha256sum withdraw_NNNN.zkey    # must equal the transcript's "output zkey sha256"
   ```

2. Verify it against the circuit and the phase-1 ptau (both from the tree;
   the coordinator confirms the ptau's BLAKE2b matches the pinned hash in the
   transcript header):

   ```
   node zk/node_modules/snarkjs/cli.js zkey verify \
     zk/build/withdraw.r1cs zk/keys/pot_hermez15.ptau withdraw_NNNN.zkey
   # must print: ZKey Ok!
   ```

   If either check fails: **stop**, do not contribute, and tell the
   coordinator. A failed check invalidates the chain, not just your hop.

## Step 2 — Prepare your entropy (read all of this first)

- Use a **long, unguessable secret that exists nowhere else**: diceware
  words, hardware-random output, keyboard mashing — **at least ~30
  characters** of real randomness.
- **NEVER** pass entropy on a command line (`-e=...`) and **NEVER** set the
  `CEREMONY_ENTROPY` environment variable. Both are rehearsal-only mechanisms;
  command lines and env vars leak via shell history, `/proc`, and crash dumps.
  The contribution script prompts you interactively with echo disabled —
  that prompt is the only place your entropy should ever be typed.
- Do not write the entropy down "temporarily". Do not put it in a password
  manager. Do not screenshot it. It exists in your head / your dice / your
  hardware output for the next few minutes, and then it must not exist at all.

## Step 3 — Contribute

From the tree root:

```
zk/ceremony/contribute.sh withdraw_NNNN.zkey withdraw_NNNN+1.zkey "<your public name>"
```

- The script prompts for your entropy twice (hidden input; the two entries
  must match).
- It refuses to overwrite an existing output file — the chain is append-only.
  If it refuses, you have the wrong output filename; ask the coordinator.
- On success it prints three things you must publish (next step): your
  **contribution hash**, the **output zkey sha256**, and your name as recorded.

## Step 4 — Publish immediately, then hand off

1. Send the coordinator (for the public transcript), right away — not at the
   end of the day:
   - your public name (as you typed it),
   - the contribution hash,
   - the output zkey's sha256.
2. Pass `withdraw_NNNN+1.zkey` to the coordinator (or directly to the next
   contributor, if the coordinator tells you to). The file is ~6 MB.

## Step 5 — Destroy your entropy

- Clear your terminal scrollback.
- Confirm the entropy exists in **no** note, file, password manager, chat
  message, or shell history.
- Tell the coordinator, in one sentence, what you did to destroy it (this
  statement goes in the transcript — e.g. "generated by diceware, typed only
  at the prompt, terminal cleared, never written down").

## Rules that invalidate the ceremony (do not improvise around them)

- One contribution per person, in sequence. Never contribute twice under two
  names; never "fix" a hop by redoing it silently — tell the coordinator.
- Never reuse entropy from anywhere else, and never use this entropy again
  for anything.
- If anything about your hop goes wrong (wrong file, failed verify, entropy
  possibly exposed), say so immediately. An aborted ceremony is cheap; a
  quietly compromised one is not.

## Questions

Ask the coordinator before you start if anything above is unclear — after
your hop is the wrong time to discover a misunderstanding.
