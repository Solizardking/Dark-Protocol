01What is Dark Protocol?

On a public blockchain, every payment leaves a trail anyone can follow. Dark Protocol breaks the trail — without asking you to trust the people running it.

Think of a cloakroom

You hand over your coat and get a ticket. Later, anyone holding a valid ticket can claim a coat — but nobody watching can tell which coat belonged to which person, because the tickets don't name anyone. Dark Protocol is a cloakroom for SOL: your deposit is the coat, a cryptographic note only you hold is the ticket, and a piece of mathematics called a zero-knowledge proof is how you show "I have a valid ticket" without revealing which deposit was yours.

The catch with every cloakroom

It only works if the cloakroom itself can't cheat: it must never hand out more coats than were checked in, never let someone claim with a forged ticket, never let the same ticket be spent twice, and never let the staff walk off with the coats. In Dark Protocol there's no staff and no office — there's only code, running on Solana, that anyone can read. So the real question is: how do we know the code actually enforces those rules? That's what the rest of this page is about.

02Testing checks examples. Proof checks everything.

Most software is tested: you try a few thousand scenarios and check the right thing happens. That catches a lot — but an attacker only needs the one scenario nobody tried.

Formal verification is different. A specialised system reads the actual program code and tries to prove, mathematically, that a rule holds for every possible sequence of actions, in any order, with any amounts — the way you'd prove a theorem about all numbers, not just the ones you tested. If it can't prove a rule, it doesn't shrug: it produces a concrete counterexample — an exact recipe of steps that breaks the rule — so the bug can be fixed and the proof run again.

On November 18, 2026, we submitted the Dark Protocol program to Aristotle, a formal-verification system built by Harmonic, under a project named dark-protocol-pilot. The run is in progress now. When it finishes, we will publish the results on this page either way — proved, or refuted with the counterexample.

03The six promises we asked it to prove

These are the exact properties in the submitted specification, translated into plain language. In cloakroom terms first, then in real terms.

1

Money is conserved

The pool can never pay out more than was put in. Every lamport is accounted for: the total paid out never exceeds the total deposited, no matter what sequence of deposits and withdrawals anyone tries — and sending money to the pool as a "donation" can't inflate what it owes.

2

No withdrawal without a valid ticket

Funds move only after the program has checked, in order: the amount is real, the deposit history being claimed against is genuine, the pool can afford it, and the zero-knowledge proof verifies. A failed check cancels the whole withdrawal — including the "ticket used" stamp, so an honest user never loses their note to a rejected attempt.

3

The amount is sealed to the note

When you deposit, the amount is cryptographically sealed into your note by the program itself — it can't be edited afterwards. This kills the classic drain: deposit a speck, then claim the note says a fortune. We already watched that attack fail live on the test network; this property asks for the proof that it can never work.

4

There is no backdoor — not even for us

The program records who deployed it, but that identity authorises nothing. The only way money ever leaves the pool is the proof-checked withdrawal path. No admin key, no operator override, no "emergency" shortcut.

5

Honest withdrawals always work

Security that locks everyone out is just a different failure. If your proof is valid, your note is unspent, and the pool holds your money, your withdrawal goes through — and if a withdrawal ever fails, the failure has a specific named reason, never a mystery. This is the property that turns "it worked in our rehearsal" into "it must work, under these stated conditions."

6

The pool's memory can't be rewritten

The pool keeps a rolling record of every deposit. That record is append-only: entries are produced by the program itself, old entries age out only after exactly 32 newer ones, and the record's summary always matches a from-scratch recount of every deposit ever made.

Plus a zeroth promise underneath all of them: on any valid input, the program never crashes or misbehaves arithmetically — no overflows, no panics, checked maths only.

04How we got here

Formal verification is the newest layer, not the only one. Dark Protocol was built the slow way:

DONE

Independent audit — 39 findings

A full audit of the original code found 39 problems, including critical ones: checks that were faked, proofs that weren't really checked. We published the findings and ordered a rewrite instead of patching over them.

DONE

Rewrite, then a hostile re-audit

The rewritten program was audited again — and the re-audit caught one more critical flaw (a deposit could be unbound from its amount: the drain in promise 3). It was fixed, and the fix is now the thing being formally proved.

DONE

Full dress rehearsal on the test network

Deposits, withdrawals, a withdrawal through a relayer, a replay attack (spending the same note twice — rejected), a tampered amount (rejected), and the 1-lamport drain attempt (failed, as designed). Every result matched the ledger to the lamport.

The setup ceremony

The privacy proofs rely on a one-time setup that must be generated in public, by several independent people each adding their own secret randomness. The mathematics guarantees the setup is sound if even one participant was honest and destroyed their secret. Our ceremony uses the public Hermez setup (54 contributors) plus five contributors of our own and a public randomness beacon. Details and the contributor list will be published with the transcript.

05What verification does not do

Honest limits, on purpose

  • It's not finished. The run is in progress. Until results are published here, "formally verified" describes the process we've started — not a result we've received.
  • It verifies the program, not the privacy circuit. The zero-knowledge circuit's soundness rests on the setup ceremony (above) and the audit trail — a different layer, checked a different way.
  • Two steps are taken on stated assumptions. The specification says openly that the proof treats the pool's hashing and the proof-checker itself as trusted building blocks, and says exactly where. If the verifier can only prove a promise under those assumptions, the result will say so — that's a feature, not fine print.
  • It says nothing about speed or cost. A separate failure class — operations too expensive to fit in a transaction — is handled by measurement and rehearsal, not proof. (It bit us once, on the test network, and the fix is part of why the rehearsal mattered.)
  • This is still a test-network system. Dark Protocol runs on Solana devnet. Mainnet comes only after the ceremony and the verification results are in — and that decision will be announced, not slipped in.

06Questions

What happens if the verifier finds a bug?

Then it did its job. A refuted promise comes back with a concrete counterexample — an exact sequence of steps that breaks the rule. We fix the code, and the fixed version gets proved again. Finding the bug in a proof is infinitely cheaper than finding it in production.

Who is Harmonic, and what is Aristotle?

Harmonic is the company behind Aristotle, an automated theorem-proving system that translates Rust programs into mathematical models and proves (or refutes) stated properties about them. In its published Solana case study, it produced 28 proved results on a production order-book program — and found two real bugs, which were fixed. That's the standard we're holding Dark Protocol to.

Did you have to hand over the code?

Yes — verification means the verifier reads the real program, all of it, exactly as deployed on the test network. The submitted bundle contained only the program source and the specification: no keys, no credentials, nothing that could move funds.

When will results be published?

When the run completes — these proofs take minutes to days. This page will be updated with the per-promise outcomes and the verifier's own statement of what it did and didn't cover.
